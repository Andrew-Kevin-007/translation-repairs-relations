\begin{abstract}
Populating knowledge bases such as Wikidata from text in low-resource
languages faces an unmeasured distinction: failure to understand the source
text versus failure to ground what was understood into canonical
knowledge-base identifiers. We test whether translating the input to
English repairs relation identification but not entity
grounding\footnote{Per the gap-recovery criterion pre-registered in
Section~\ref{sec:instrument}.}, using two open 8B-class instruction-tuned
LLMs, Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, across eight Indic languages in
\textsc{direct} and \textsc{pivot} (translate-then-extract via
IndicTrans2~\cite{gala2023indictrans2}) arms, with component-level scoring
that separates relation and entity errors, against a benchmark we adapt by
deterministically re-grounding an existing fact-to-text alignment
resource's~\cite{abhishek2022xalign} English fact labels in Wikidata,
validated by automatic cross-checking against the live knowledge base and a
partial native-speaker check. The frozen decision table returns a mixed
verdict:
translation improves relation-F1 for Qwen3-8B in every language and for
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in seven of eight (unclipped gap recovery $+0.67$ to
$+1.85$ over the five Qwen3-8B languages with a measurable baseline gap;
exact one-sided sign test $p=.031$, not surviving the conservative family
correction), while entity grounding presents no verdict-eligible gap, the
English control itself near-floor. For Qwen3-8B, translation instead
reduces entity-F1 in seven of eight languages; an archived-candidate
decomposition attributes this to retrieval rather than selection (the gold
entity is chosen in 92.9--99.5\% of calls once retrieved), a pattern
specific to Qwen3-8B and not replicated by Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
(76.2--94.7\%, inverted retrieval direction). The automated validation bound
supports the pre-registered reliability claim for gold reuse: at most
4.8\% (pooled 1.3\%) of predictions absent from the adapted gold are true
in the live knowledge base. We release the benchmark, audit protocol, and
evaluation harness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: The Hypothesis and Why It Matters}\label{sec:intro}
% First paragraph = the hypothesis, verbatim from the pre-registration prose form.
% No pipeline advertising. Banned vocabulary list: PREREGISTRATION.md section 9.

This paper tests one pre-registered hypothesis: \emph{the low-resource penalty
in populating Wikidata from Indic-language text lies in entity
canonicalization, not relation understanding}, that translating the input to
English repairs relation identification but not entity grounding (we use
\emph{entity canonicalization} and \emph{entity grounding} interchangeably:
resolving an extracted mention to its Wikidata identifier, or QID). The
hypothesis, its decision thresholds, and the outcome-to-claim mapping were frozen in a
public pre-registration before any model output was scored
(Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).

This distinction matters because the two failure modes call for opposite
remedies. Failures of \emph{understanding} are remedied by
better multilingual pretraining or inference-time translation; failures of
\emph{canonicalization} are not, since a model can understand a translated
sentence perfectly and still be unable to name the QID of an entity it has
only ever seen written in another script. Standard evaluation scores
extracted triples monolithically, so a missed triple never says which
problem it was; every scored error here is attributable to one component or
the other by design.

The eight languages studied (Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada,
Malayalam, Marathi, and Gujarati, plus an English control slice) are spoken
by over a billion people while remaining, by knowledge-base coverage and NLP
resource standards, low-resource~\cite{joshi2020stateandfate}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~A text-to-triple extraction benchmark for eight Indic languages plus an
English control, built by reversing XAlign's~\cite{abhishek2022xalign}
manually annotated test split and deterministically re-grounding its
English fact labels into Wikidata identifiers, with per-language coverage
and drop reasons released with the harness (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).
(2)~A validation of that benchmark as an instrument: automated live-Wikidata
cross-checking of every scored prediction plus a screened native-speaker
check for one language, yielding a quantified gold-incompleteness lower
bound (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).
(3)~A pre-registered, component-separated measurement of what translation
does and does not repair in low-resource KG population, with
hierarchical-bootstrap uncertainty and a frozen decision table
(Sections~\ref{sec:test} and~\ref{sec:secondary}); the measured outcome is
the mixed branch described above.

\subsection{Aim and Objectives}\label{sec:aims}
Three further objectives run through the paper beyond the primary
hypothesis: decomposing entity-grounding failure into retrieval versus
selection
(Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-decomp}); quantifying uncertainty with a
hierarchical bootstrap under a Holm-Bonferroni-corrected rule
(Sections~\ref{sec:grr}--\ref{sec:decision}); and examining, exploratorily,
whether the dissociation holds across the study's language families
(Section~\ref{sec:grouping}).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Multilingual relation extraction and KG population.}
Supervised multilingual relation extraction is anchored by
RED\textsuperscript{FM}~\cite{huguetcabot2023redfm} and its mREBEL
extractor, whose training covers only Hindi of the eight languages here
(Section~\ref{sec:secondary}). Earlier monolingual end-to-end
extraction~\cite{huguetcabot2021rebel} does not separate relation
identification from entity grounding; LLM-era extraction reframes it as
constrained generation against a
schema~\cite{josifoski2022genie} or revisits classical RE with LLM
prompting~\cite{wadhwa2023revisiting}, neither decomposing failure by
component.

\textbf{Entity linking against Wikidata.}
Entity canonicalization here grounds mentions in
Wikidata~\cite{vrandecic2014wikidata}, an instance of multilingual entity
linking's retrieve-then-rerank design~\cite{wu2020blink}, where
Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-decomp} shows this study's failures concentrate. Autoregressive and cross-lingual linking
systems~\cite{decao2022mgenre} and large-scale
evaluations~\cite{botha2020entitylinking100} report quality degrading with
language coverage independent of the model itself, and a Wikidata-specific
survey finds most approaches lean on labels and descriptions
alone~\cite{moller2022wikidatasurvey}, the interface this paper's
candidate search measures.

\textbf{Fact--text resources for Indic languages.}
XAlign~\cite{abhishek2022xalign} aligns Wikidata facts with sentences in
Indic languages for \emph{fact-to-text generation} (this study uses the
v2.0 release, which adds Malayalam) and includes a manually annotated test
set. We use it in the reversed direction (sentences
as input, aligned facts as extraction gold), a construction that is ours,
not the original authors', whose validity we treat as a claim requiring
evidence (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}); its reliability for reuse as
extraction gold is itself a pre-registered secondary question.

\textbf{Translate-test versus direct multilingual inference.}
Translate-test entered cross-lingual NLP as a baseline that translating test
data outperforms other options~\cite{conneau2018xnli}, became standard
protocol across dozens of languages and tasks~\cite{ahuja2023mega},
and persists in the LLM era, where models underuse their multilingual
capacity when prompted in non-English languages~\cite{etxaniz2024think}.
That literature evaluates end-to-end
scores, not \emph{which component} translation repairs, and can understate a
real risk, translation itself introduces artifacts that change what is
measured~\cite{artetxe2020translationartifacts} (Section~\ref{sec:implications}).
Our design imports the comparison into KG population and decomposes it,
using IndicTrans2~\cite{gala2023indictrans2} (all 22 scheduled Indian
languages) plus a pre-registered CometKiwi~\cite{rei2022cometkiwi}
quality-estimation check relating residual \textsc{pivot}-arm error to MT
quality, not run in this study (Section~\ref{sec:secondary}).

\subsection{Problem Statement}\label{sec:problem}
The literature above leaves a specific, testable gap. End-to-end
extractors and multilingual entity-linking evaluations alike report one
aggregate
score~\cite{huguetcabot2023redfm,huguetcabot2021rebel,wu2020blink,botha2020entitylinking100},
and translate-test evidence that English translation improves multilingual
inference~\cite{etxaniz2024think} is reported the same way, so neither a
missed fact's component (relation versus entity), nor, on the entity side,
its sub-mechanism (retrieval versus disambiguation), nor which sub-task of
a structured extraction pipeline actually benefits is separately visible.
Fact-to-text alignment resources built for generation, such as
XAlign~\cite{abhishek2022xalign}, are attractive to reuse as extraction
gold but carry no established reliability estimate for that direction, and
a further proposed driver of low-resource gaps, tokenizer fertility (up to
fifteen times more tokens for the same text in some
languages~\cite{petrov2023tokenizers}), is rarely tested jointly with a
component-level failure decomposition.

\subsection{Distinction from Prior Work}\label{sec:distinction}
This study proposes no new extraction system and no hybrid of the two LLMs
studied; it proposes a measurement design, positioned against prior work on
dimensions rather than accuracy, since no existing system is evaluated on
this benchmark under comparable conditions.
RED\textsuperscript{FM}/mREBEL~\cite{huguetcabot2023redfm}
performs extraction only, with one supervised seq2seq model, covers one of
the eight study languages, and is neither pre-registered nor tested for gold
reliability; this study evaluates extraction and canonicalization together,
separates the relation and entity contributions, covers all eight languages
with two open local 8B systems under a frozen decision table, and validates
its own benchmark as an instrument (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).
XAlign~\cite{abhishek2022xalign} is a fact-to-text generation resource over
Indic languages, evaluated for that direction only; this study
supplies the reverse direction and the reliability evaluation neither prior
work provides.

\section{The Instrument: Benchmark and Its Validation}\label{sec:instrument}

\subsection{From Alignment Resource to Extraction Benchmark}
The manually annotated XAlign test split provides, per language, sentence-level
alignments between native-language Wikipedia text and Wikidata facts rendered
as English labels (subject, predicate, object, plus qualifiers); a
native-speaker review of the Tamil slice found source fluency uneven, with
frequent machine-translation-like phrasing (inconsistent transliteration,
literal calendar constructions, templated boilerplate). Two transformations
produce an extraction benchmark from it: \emph{reversal},
treating the sentence as input and its aligned facts as the triples an
extractor should recover (by construction a set of facts genuinely asserted
by the text, since the original annotation asks which facts the sentence
expresses, while inheriting XAlign's human verification), and
\emph{re-grounding}, resolving XAlign's English label strings to the
canonical Wikidata identifiers scoring requires.

The re-grounding procedure, released as source in the supplementary harness
(\texttt{gold\_canonicalize.py}), runs without LLM participation and is
deterministic in the reproducible sense: given the archived Wikidata
response cache and the released predicate mapping table, it yields the same
benchmark exactly. Predicate labels
resolve against Wikidata's property registry by exact case-folded match,
with a 5-entry manually verified override table for the small closed set
whose labels have drifted since XAlign's snapshot (released with
per-predicate match types); a lone non-exact search candidate is accepted
but flagged for post-hoc review. Subjects resolve by retrieving name
candidates and scoring each by how many of the item's own gold facts its
live Wikidata claims corroborate. Objects are never resolved by free
search: an entity object's QID
is accepted only if it appears among the resolved subject's own claim values
for that property with a matching English label, while date and quantity
objects are normalized to a literal and accepted only if that literal
matches a claim value, then retained as a literal rather than a QID. An item
enters the benchmark only if \emph{every} fact resolves (strict retention),
so the benchmark never carries silently missing gold into recall
denominators; membership in Wikidata is thereby part of the task definition,
and NIL detection (entities Wikidata lacks) is out of scope by construction.

Table~\ref{tab:gold} reports per-language retention, from 85.5\% (Kannada) to
92.5\% (Hindi); full drop-reason counts are released with the harness. This
loss is dominated by knowledge-base drift rather than resolution failures
(confirmed below). Differential retention shifts the composition of \emph{cross-language}
comparisons (a caveat those analyses carry explicitly) but, because the
primary contrast is item-paired within each language (Section~\ref{sec:test}),
cannot confound the within-language \textsc{direct}-versus-\textsc{pivot}
contrast.

\subsection{Component Metrics and Three-Way Prediction Classification}
Every run is scored with relation-F1 (property identifiers only), entity-F1
(subject and object QIDs only), and full-triple F1, computed in the strict
(Wikidata-ID) form throughout. A relaxed, surface-form variant of the same
three metrics was pre-registered but not computed for this draft, the same
treatment the paper gives other un-run pre-registered items
(Section~\ref{sec:secondary}); nothing below depends on it. Separating the
relation and entity components is what allows the hypothesis to be tested at
all: a triple wrong only in its object QID counts against entity grounding,
not relation understanding.

Because gold reuse can understate model precision (a model may extract a
true fact the aligned gold simply does not contain), every predicted triple
is classified three ways: in gold (exact tuple match); absent from gold but
verifiable as a live Wikidata statement (gold incompleteness, credited in
adjusted precision), checked against the same cached Wikidata access layer
as re-grounding under literal-matching rules frozen with the prompts before
any scoring; or absent from Wikidata entirely (a hallucination candidate,
analyzed taxonomically in Section~\ref{sec:secondary}). The exact procedure
is released with the harness (\texttt{wikidata\_check.py}). Predictions
lacking a resolvable subject QID, property PID, or object are set aside
before this split (pooled across all 34 cells, 19.4\% of predictions);
already scored as errors under
the primary metrics, their exclusion affects only this diagnostic's
denominator, not precision.

\subsection{Instrument Validation}
Two independent checks estimate how far the adapted gold can be trusted. A
native-speaker audit protocol, released with the benchmark, specifies
coverage of one full language subset (601 retained Tamil items of 656
source, Table~\ref{tab:gold}) and two judgments per item against written
guidelines: whether each gold fact is genuinely asserted by the sentence
(gold validity), and which sentence-asserted facts are absent from the gold
(gold incompleteness, the miss rate claim C2 tests), each cross-checked
against Wikidata; the auditor is treated as a measurement device, with the
miss-rate threshold (5\%) fixed in the pre-registration.
The automated class-(ii) rate separately provides a scalable per-language
estimate of gold incompleteness for all eight languages. The pre-registration
commits to publishing whichever way these measurements fall: a miss rate at
or above threshold, together with a positive automated class-(ii) rate in at
least seven of eight languages, supports the claim that generation-oriented
gold understates extraction precision; a miss rate below threshold supports
the pre-registered inverse claim that such reuse is reliable.

Under the submission deadline, the full per-item protocol was not executed;
instead, an automatic candidate screen proposed, across all 601 retained
items, any relation the sentence may assert that the gold lacks, and a
native speaker adjudicated all 25 items the screen surfaced, confirming 14
gold-incomplete (screen recall 11/14 on adjudicated
items, a false-negative rate of about 21\%; Amendment~A6). Nationality
adjectives were counted only when attributed to the person, not when
modifying an office or institution (e.g.\ ``Singapore's prime minister'').
Adjudicated gold-incomplete items: 14 of 601 (2.33\%), a lower bound rather
than an estimate: the remaining 576 items were never individually reviewed,
and measured screen recall is 11/14. The pre-registered $\geq$5\% threshold
(30/601 $=$ 4.99\% falls short; 31/601 $=$ 5.16\%) is not licensed on this
figure alone; C2's reliability determination instead rests on the automated
class-(ii) bound below. A pre-specified 100-item completion sample over the
unadjudicated items was drawn, judged, and discarded as unusable; no
further native-speaker sampling was performed before submission
(Amendment~A7). Two further gold-validity errors surfaced in
passing: \texttt{ta-test-0029}'s residence (California) over-specifies a
sentence asserting only birth in Los Angeles; \texttt{ta-test-0516}'s
education (University of Manchester) over-specifies a sentence describing
continuing research there, an employment relation, not a degree.

The automated estimate came back decisively below threshold: across all 34
(system, arm, language) cells, the share of not-in-gold predictions
verifiable as live Wikidata statements never reaches 5\% (maximum 4.8\%, one
cell; median 1.3\%; pooled 389 of 30{,}171, 1.3\%), so what the models
predict beyond gold is overwhelmingly not knowledge the gold failed to
record. A
drop-classification audit of the retention step points the same way: in a
pre-registered 50-item sample of dropped source items, all 50 are consistent
with knowledge-base drift, and exactly one item in the full 660-drop pool
exhibits the procedural signature the classification was designed to catch
(an unparsed unit quantity).

\subsection{Arms and Evaluation Pipeline}\label{sec:arms}
Each system runs \textsc{direct} (original language in,
extract-then-canonicalize) and \textsc{pivot} (translation to English via
IndicTrans2's distilled \texttt{indic-en-dist-200M} checkpoint, then the
identical English pipeline) over the same low-resource items, item-paired by
construction, plus the \textsc{english} control slice, which by construction
contains different sentences (pairing note, Section~\ref{sec:grr}). Each
item passes through an arm-specific input transform, open-vocabulary
extraction with the response archived before any parsing, and per-triple
canonicalization against retrieved candidates (the full per-item procedure
is released as \texttt{run\_matrix.py} with the harness). Using the smallest
released IndicTrans2 checkpoint rather than its 1B flagship means the
relation-repair result below is a \emph{lower bound} on what a stronger
pivot engine would deliver, and correspondingly that the entity-side damage
cannot be claimed independent of MT quality (Section~\ref{sec:implications}).

A mechanical parsing artifact surfaced at first scoring: Llama-3.1 echoes
the canonicalization template's header line before answering, handled with
a dated pre-reparse amendment (both pre- and post-amendment scores
released). Relation canonicalization is closed-set: the
canonicalization prompt carries the inventory of properties attested in the
adapted gold, so systems \emph{select} rather than generate property
identifiers, and absolute relation-F1 is therefore not open-inventory
performance (Section~\ref{sec:implications}); extraction itself remains
open-vocabulary.

\subsection{Entity Retrieval versus Selection}\label{sec:retrieval-decomp}
Entity canonicalization is retrieval-mediated: a mention is grounded by
choosing among at most eight candidates returned by Wikidata's search API
(queried in the source language with an English top-up in \textsc{direct},
and in English in \textsc{pivot} and \textsc{english}), with the instruction
prompts themselves held constant in English across all arms and languages;
only the passage varies. Retrieval is thus a component of measured entity
grounding, not a controlled-away nuisance: a native-script mention can fail
at retrieval before the model ever chooses, an asymmetry presumed
conservative for the hypothesis at design time. We decompose entity errors,
per system, arm, and language, into subject recall@8 (whether the gold
subject QID appears among the archived retrieved candidates) and selection
given retrieved (whether the model's chosen QID equals gold, conditional on
that retrieval having succeeded).

The decomposition inverts the presumed direction of the retrieval asymmetry
for Qwen3-8B, the system driving the paper's usable relation-repair
contrasts: Figure~\ref{fig:retrieval} shows subject-candidate recall@8
\emph{higher} in \textsc{direct} than in \textsc{pivot} for seven of eight
languages (Kannada the sole exception, at parity), the native-language query
with English top-up retrieving gold entities that English-side search on
translated mentions loses. Llama-3.1-8B does not replicate this direction,
its \textsc{pivot} recall@8 exceeds \textsc{direct} in seven of eight
languages (Marathi the sole exception), so the mechanism is evidenced for
one system and does not generalize across both by construction. Selection,
by contrast, is high wherever retrieval succeeds in both systems, though not
uniformly near-ceiling: given the gold entity among the candidates,
Qwen3-8B selects it in 92.9--99.5\% of calls per cell, and Llama-3.1-8B in
76.2--94.7\%. Entity grounding here is therefore retrieval-bound rather than
selection-bound for the system whose behavior licenses the paper's
mechanistic claim, and the \textsc{english} control is itself
retrieval-limited (subject recall@8 $=.549$, Qwen3-8B).

\subsection{Gap Recovery Ratio}\label{sec:grr}
For metric $m \in \{\text{relation-F1}, \text{entity-F1}\}$ and each
(language, system) cell,
\[
\mathrm{GRR}_m \;=\; \frac{m_{\textsc{pivot}} - m_{\textsc{direct}}}
                          {m_{\textsc{english}} - m_{\textsc{direct}}},
\]
reported unclipped (a clipped-to-$[0,1]$ variant was pre-registered but is
not in the released score report; nothing below depends on it). Cells whose
baseline gap $(m_{\textsc{english}} - m_{\textsc{direct}})$ is under 8 F1
points are excluded from verdicts as underpowered (logged and reported); if
a metric retains fewer than five usable languages, its verdict is declared
underpowered and only CIs are reported. That rule and the per-cell verdict
test of Section~\ref{sec:decision} are released as \texttt{score.py};
uncertainty comes from
a hierarchical bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples, 95\% CIs, released
as \texttt{bootstrap.py}): item-paired for \textsc{pivot}-vs-\textsc{direct},
independent resampling for the unpaired \textsc{english} term. Because the
\textsc{english} ceiling uses XAlign's English slice (same domain and
register, different sentences), the design additionally pre-registers a
synthetic-parallel robustness probe (machine-translating English gold
\emph{into} each study language for a fully paired denominator check,
translationese caveat stated); prepared but not run for this draft
(Section~\ref{sec:implications}).

\subsection{Decision Table and Verdict}\label{sec:decision}
The pre-registered table maps repair patterns to claims. In brief: relation
repair requires $\mathrm{GRR} \geq 0.75$ with the residual
(\textsc{english}$-$\textsc{pivot}) CI including 0; non-repair is
$\mathrm{GRR} < 0.35$ or an improvement CI including 0; the intermediate band
($0.35 \leq \mathrm{GRR} < 0.75$ with the improvement CI excluding 0) is
scored as partial repair. The hypothesis is confirmed only when relation-F1
repairs while entity-F1 either does not repair or repairs only partially, and
the asymmetry $\mathrm{GRR}_{\text{rel}} - \mathrm{GRR}_{\text{ent}} \geq 0.30$
with a CI excluding 0. Cross-language consistency uses an exact one-sided
sign test in the hypothesis' pre-specified direction (all-but-at-most-one of
the usable languages, exact binomial $p<0.05$; sidedness fixed by a dated,
pre-scoring amendment recorded in the frozen file's log). The primary family
(2 metrics $\times$ 2 systems = 4 tests) is Holm-Bonferroni corrected; system
disagreement on a verdict row resolves to the weaker outcome. Both
falsification branches carry their own pre-registered claims: repair of
\emph{both} components would evidence an English-bound knowledge interface
in current open LLMs; relation-F1 itself failing to repair redirects the
explanation toward entity-coverage structure, tested by popularity-quartile
stratification over the retained items' popularity distribution (dropped
items are unscorable, so the quartile claim is scoped to what the benchmark
retains). Any other outcome is reported as mixed: the continuous GRR profile
is published without forcing a headline verdict, led instead by the
gold-reliability finding (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Usable cells.} The 8-point gap rule excludes more of the grid than
anticipated, for two informative reasons. On entity-F1, the \textsc{english}
ceiling is itself near the floor (Qwen3 $.098$, Llama-3.1 $.143$). Entity
grounding is retrieval-bound even in English, so no language shows a
measurable entity gap for Qwen3 (0 usable; Llama-3.1: 4, below the 5-language
bar). On relation-F1, Llama-3.1's \textsc{direct} scores sit above, at, or
within the 8-point exclusion band of its own English ceiling in every
language (e.g., Bengali $.609$ vs.\ $.515$ above; Kannada $.449$ vs.\ $.515$
within-band), leaving no language with a measurable gap to repair (0 usable).
Both metrics for Llama-3.1 and entity-F1 for Qwen3 therefore fall to the
pre-registered escape hatch: CIs published, no verdict. The one
metric--system pair with a testable repair question is Qwen3 relation-F1,
with five usable languages (Table~\ref{tab:grr}).

\paragraph{Relation repair.} Gujarati, Marathi, and Telugu meet the full
\textsc{repaired} criterion ($\mathrm{GRR} \geq 0.75$ with the residual
\textsc{english}$-$\textsc{pivot} CI including 0); Kannada is
\textsc{partial} (improvement CI $[+.076,+.143]$ excludes 0). Malayalam exceeds the
taxonomy: its residual CI excludes zero on the \emph{favorable} side
($[-.142,-.063]$); \textsc{pivot}'s point estimate lands above the English
ceiling with a residual CI excluding zero, a case the frozen three-way rule
did not anticipate; we report it as beyond-\textsc{repaired} under any
monotone reading and count it toward no claim it would strengthen.
Cross-language consistency holds, family-uncorrected: all five usable
languages move in the hypothesized direction (exact one-sided $p=.031$),
not family-significant under the frozen four-test Holm family on the
conservative reading that unrunnable tests still occupy the
family\footnote{$0.03125\times4=0.125$. On the alternative reading that the
family comprises only the tests that could run, it is significant; we adopt
the conservative reading for every claim in this paper and report the
liberal one only here.}.

\paragraph{Verdict.} With entity-F1 underpowered on both systems, the
confirmation criterion cannot be evaluated, and neither falsification branch
triggers ($\mathrm{GRR}_{\text{rel}}$ is not below $0.35$ anywhere usable;
both-repair cannot be assessed). The pre-registered outcome is therefore the
\emph{mixed} branch: the continuous profile above is published without a
forced headline verdict, and the paper leads with the gold-reliability
finding (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}). Descriptively (outside the verdict
machinery), the direction of the dissociation is uniform: \textsc{pivot}
improves relation-F1 in 8/8 languages on Qwen3 (7/8 on Llama-3.1) while
\emph{reducing} entity-F1 in 7/8 (Qwen3, e.g.\ Hindi $.216 \to .112$).
Llama-3.1's entity-F1 direction is an exact 4-of-8 split, pivot exceeds
direct in half the languages, which is the cleanest evidence available that
the entity effect is Qwen-specific rather than shared by both systems; the
retrieval decomposition above locates the Qwen3 entity-side damage in the
translated query's weaker retrieval, not in model selection.
Figure~\ref{fig:comparison} extends this picture descriptively to all eight
languages and both systems (the not-yet-run synthetic-parallel probe bounds
only the \textsc{english}-ceiling pairing caveat, not the within-language
contrasts).

\section{Secondary Analyses}\label{sec:secondary}
% C3 gate resolved to drop (mREBEL is Hindi-only); C5 fragment and the
% QE-correlation sharpening check not run (omitted, not partial); C6
% hallucination-candidate structure reported descriptively below.

\paragraph{Popularity stratification (pre-registered for the B3 branch;
reported descriptively).} Entity-occurrence error rates in the \textsc{pivot}
arm are flat across sitelink-popularity (the count of Wikimedia sitelinks
the entity has, a notability proxy) quartiles of the retained inventory:
bottom-vs-top ratio $1.03$ (Qwen3; $.706$ vs.\ $.688$) and $0.89$
(Llama-3.1), far from the $1.5$ criterion. Failures do not concentrate on
obscure entities (famous ones fail almost as often), consistent with the
retrieval-bound account and not with an entity-coverage account.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and composition checks (pre-registered).} Excluding
every item touching the two audit-flagged predicate mappings
(P112, P118) shifts no usable GRR by more than $0.05$ (largest shift:
Marathi, $1.334 \to 1.293$). The retention-vs-GRR association across the
five usable cells is $\rho = 0.60$ (n$=5$, descriptive only, stated with the
retention-differential caveat). GRR CI half-widths run $0.12$--$0.16$
(Llama-3.1 cells) and $0.22$--$0.54$ (Qwen3 relation cells, wide where GRR is
large).

\paragraph{Supervised-baseline inversion test (C3).} The pre-registered scope
gate recorded a drop: mREBEL, the supervised baseline, has training coverage
that includes only Hindi of the eight study languages
(Section~\ref{sec:related}), so an inversion test against it would be
uninformative for seven of the eight comparisons.

\paragraph{Exploratory items not executed.} Three further pre-registered
exploratory items were not run in this study and are omitted rather than
partially reported: the C4 tokenizer-fertility-versus-resource-proxy
correlate, the C5 fragment on repair-pattern stability across model scale,
and the QE-correlation sharpening check relating CometKiwi estimates to
residual \textsc{pivot}-arm error.

\paragraph{Hallucination-candidate structure (C6).} Of the 30{,}171 pooled
not-in-gold predictions (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}), those beyond the 389
verified as true live-Wikidata statements are hallucination candidates;
their taxonomic structure, released with the error tables, is descriptive,
not a claim.

\subsection{Language-Family Grouping (Exploratory)}\label{sec:grouping}
This grouping analysis is exploratory and post-hoc: language family, script,
and resource tier are not pre-registered variables, no amendment governs it,
and it contributes to no verdict; it is reported because the null result is
informative. Splitting the eight languages into Indo-Aryan and Dravidian
families, an exact 4-versus-4 permutation test over all 70 relabelings finds nothing: the smallest $p$ across
20 such tests is $.1143$, against a minimum attainable two-sided $p$ of
$.0286$ at this sample size, and the dissociation itself is far from
separating by family ($p=.771$ Qwen3, $p=.543$ Llama-3.1). What does
correlate with entity grounding is retrieval coverage, not genealogy:
subject recall@8 correlates with entity-F1 at Spearman $\rho=+.881$,
$+.738$, $+.738$, and $+.857$ across the four system-arm combinations,
stratifying the languages into high- and low-retrieval groups that cut
across both families, independently corroborating the paper's
retrieval-bound mechanism, consistent with multilingual entity linking
degrading with language coverage independent of the linking
model~\cite{botha2020entitylinking100}, named-entity scarcity in
Sub-Saharan African languages~\cite{adelani2021masakhaner}, and the
low-resource machine-translation landscape the pivot arm depends
on~\cite{nllbteam2024scaling}. We did not add a language: XAlign's three
further annotated languages (Assamese, Odia, Punjabi) are all Indo-Aryan;
the Indic family's other two
branches~\cite{doddapaneni2023indicxtreme} have no aligned split
in XAlign, and any other resource would impose a different annotation
scheme and property inventory.

\section{Implications and Limitations}\label{sec:implications}
% For practitioners (translate first?) and benchmark builders (gold-reuse reliability).
% Limitations: single-family breadth, gold reuse caveats, audit on one language.

The measured profile yields a component-level rule for practitioners rather
than a blanket one. Where \emph{which relations} a low-resource text asserts
is the quantity of interest, translating first is worth it. Where
\emph{which entities} are involved, translation is the wrong lever for the
system whose retrieval pattern we measured; effort is better spent on the
retrieval interface itself (native-label indexing, cross-lingual entity
search), as selection accuracy given retrieval is high for both systems
(Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-decomp}).
For benchmark builders: generation-oriented
alignment gold can plausibly be reused for extraction evaluation if it is
strictly re-grounded against the live KB first, at the measured cost of a
$\sim$11\% drop rate that is overwhelmingly KB drift, with precision
estimates our automated incompleteness bound suggests are not materially
understated, consistent with but not independently confirmed by the
partial Tamil adjudication (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).

\emph{Limitations.} The study covers open LLMs of a single size class under
4-bit quantization; conclusions about scaling rest only on the labeled
exploratory fragment. Both models were served at a 4096-token context, well
under Qwen3-8B's native window and Llama-3.1's (Table~\ref{tab:params}); the
limit was binding on at least one item, forcing the compact candidate
rendering used uniformly across arms, and Qwen3's thinking mode was disabled
for latency parity with Llama-3.1, so its scores here are not comparable to
published thinking-mode Qwen3 benchmarks. The gold inherits XAlign's
person-biography domain and its 2020--2021 snapshot, mitigated but not
eliminated by strict retention and live-Wikidata cross-checking. The
native-speaker check covers one language and, within it, only the 25 items
an automatic screen surfaced of 601 (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}); the
remaining 576 Tamil items and the other seven languages rely on the
automated incompleteness estimate. A related, unexcluded confound affects
the relation-repair result only: if part of the Tamil source was itself
translated from English, the \textsc{pivot} arm's step back to English
partially round-trips it, and recovered English could sit closer to the
original phrasing than natively authored text would. The native-speaker
review shows only that fluency is uneven, not any sentence's provenance, so
this is unexcluded, not demonstrated, and does not touch the entity-side
result, whose retrieval-bound mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-decomp})
is independent of source fluency. The \textsc{pivot} arm uses one MT engine,
IndicTrans2's distilled 200M checkpoint rather than its 1B flagship
(Section~\ref{sec:arms}); the QE correlation would bound, but was not run to
remove, MT-engine and MT-quality specificity. Relation canonicalization
is scored as selection over a closed property
inventory (Section~\ref{sec:arms}), so absolute relation-F1 does not
transfer to open-world settings, though the closed set leaves the arm and
language \emph{contrasts} untouched. The
Llama-3.1 template-echo artifact (Section~\ref{sec:arms}) shows
format-compliance parsing is itself an instrument risk for cross-model
comparison. The synthetic-parallel denominator probe (Section~\ref{sec:grr})
was not run in time for this draft; until it runs, the
\textsc{english}-ceiling pairing caveat rests on the within-language pairing
argument alone. The language-family grouping (Section~\ref{sec:grouping}) is
exploratory and severely underpowered; it should not be read as a test of
any family-level hypothesis. Finally, each cell reflects a single seeded
decoding run rather than repeated sampling, so the results carry no estimate
of decoding variance.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This study asked whether the low-resource penalty in extracting Wikidata
triples from Indic-language text is a failure of relation understanding or
of entity canonicalization. The
evidence dissociates the two: translation repairs relation identification
(Qwen3-8B, five usable languages, $p=.031$ uncorrected; descriptively 7/8 for
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) but presents no verdict-eligible entity-grounding gap
under the frozen rule, and descriptively reduces Qwen3-8B's entity-F1 in 7/8
languages via weaker retrieval rather than worse selection, a mechanism
specific to Qwen3-8B (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-decomp}). The benchmark
this measurement relies on is a reuse of generation-oriented alignment gold
whose reliability we treated as a claim requiring evidence: the automated
cross-check keeps the not-in-gold-but-true rate below the pre-registered
5\% threshold in every cell.

\emph{Future work.} Direct extensions are the synthetic-parallel probe and
CometKiwi correlation (prepared but not run), a model-size sweep testing
whether the Qwen-specific mechanism reflects model family or scale, and
repeated sampling to add decoding variance to the uncertainty budget. More
broadly, the retrieval-bound account suggests a native-script-aware entity
index as an engineering target beyond what translation alone can repair.
