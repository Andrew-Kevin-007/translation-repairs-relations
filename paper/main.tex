\begin{abstract}
Populating knowledge bases such as Wikidata from text in low-resource
languages is limited by an unmeasured distinction: do failures come from
models not understanding the source text, or from models failing to ground
what they understood into canonical knowledge-base identifiers? We test
whether translating the input to English repairs relation identification but
not entity grounding\footnote{Per the gap-recovery criterion pre-registered
in Section~\ref{sec:instrument}.}, using two open 8B-class instruction-tuned
LLMs, Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (4-bit Q4\_K\_M GGUF, seeded greedy
decoding), across eight Indic languages in two arms, direct extraction and
translate-then-extract via IndicTrans2~\cite{gala2023indictrans2}, with
component-level scoring that separates relation and entity errors. We adapt
the manually annotated portion of an existing fact-to-text alignment
resource~\cite{abhishek2022xalign} into a text-to-triple extraction benchmark
by deterministically re-grounding its English fact labels in Wikidata,
validated by a native-speaker audit and automatic cross-checking against the
live knowledge base. The frozen decision table returns a mixed verdict:
translation improves relation-F1 in every language for Qwen3-8B and seven of
eight for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (unclipped gap recovery $+0.67$ to $+1.85$
over the five Qwen3-8B languages with a measurable baseline gap; exact
one-sided sign test $p=.031$, not surviving the conservative family
correction), while entity grounding presents no verdict-eligible gap under
the frozen rule, the English control is itself near-floor. For Qwen3-8B,
translation instead reduces entity-F1 in seven of eight languages; an
archived-candidate decomposition attributes this to retrieval rather than
selection (models choose the gold entity in 92.9--99.5\% of calls once
retrieval surfaces it), a pattern specific to Qwen3-8B and not replicated by
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (76.2--94.7\%, inverted retrieval direction). The
automated validation bound supports the pre-registered reliability claim for
gold reuse, pending native-speaker confirmation: at most 4.8\% (pooled
1.3\%) of predictions absent from the adapted gold are true in the live
knowledge base. We release the benchmark, audit protocol, and evaluation
harness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: The Hypothesis and Why It Matters}\label{sec:intro}
% First paragraph = the hypothesis, verbatim from the pre-registration prose form.
% No pipeline advertising. Banned vocabulary list: PREREGISTRATION.md section 9.

This paper tests one pre-registered hypothesis: \emph{the low-resource penalty
in populating Wikidata from Indic-language text lies in entity
canonicalization, not relation understanding}, that translating the input to
English repairs relation identification but not entity grounding (we use
\emph{entity canonicalization} and \emph{entity grounding} interchangeably:
resolving an extracted mention to its Wikidata identifier). The hypothesis,
its decision thresholds, and the outcome-to-claim mapping were frozen in a
public pre-registration before any model output was scored
(Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).

This distinction matters because the two failure modes call for opposite
remedies. Knowledge-graph population from text is at minimum two coupled
tasks: recognizing that a sentence asserts a relation, and grounding that
relation's participants into canonical identifiers (Wikidata QIDs) the
knowledge base can store. Failures of \emph{understanding} are remedied by
better multilingual pretraining or inference-time translation; failures of
\emph{canonicalization} are not, since a model can understand a translated
sentence perfectly and still be unable to name the QID of an entity it has
only ever seen written in another script. These remedies are funded,
engineered, and benchmarked by different communities, yet standard
evaluation scores extracted triples monolithically, so a missed triple never
says which community's problem it was; every scored error here is
attributable to one component or the other by design.

The eight languages studied (Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada,
Malayalam, Marathi, and Gujarati, plus an English control slice) are spoken
by over a billion people while remaining, by knowledge-base coverage and NLP
resource standards, low-resource~\cite{joshi2020stateandfate}: coverage of
locally prominent entities shapes what downstream systems (search, question
answering, fact verification) can know at all.

Concretely, we run Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Section~\ref{sec:test})
over the same items in two arms, \textsc{direct} (source-language extraction)
and \textsc{pivot} (IndicTrans2 translation to
English~\cite{gala2023indictrans2}, then the identical English pipeline),
against an \textsc{english} control ceiling. Scoring separates relation-F1
from entity-F1, and the pre-registered \emph{gap recovery ratio} (GRR;
Section~\ref{sec:test}) measures, per component, how much of the
low-resource-to-English gap translation closes. A decision table frozen
before data maps every outcome, including those that falsify the hypothesis,
to a designated claim, so the headline was determined by the results rather
than selected after them.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~A text-to-triple extraction benchmark for eight Indic languages plus an
English control, constructed by reversing the manually annotated test split of
XAlign~\cite{abhishek2022xalign} and deterministically re-grounding its
English fact labels into Wikidata identifiers, with the adaptation's coverage
reported per language and drop reasons released with the harness
(Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).
(2)~A validation of that benchmark as an instrument: a native-speaker audit of
one full language subset plus automated live-Wikidata cross-checking of every
scored prediction, yielding a quantified estimate of gold miss rate
(Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).
(3)~A pre-registered, component-separated measurement of what translation does
and does not repair in low-resource KG population, with hierarchical-bootstrap
uncertainty and a frozen decision table (Sections~\ref{sec:test}
and~\ref{sec:secondary}). The measured outcome is the mixed branch: for the
one system with a measurable relation-F1 gap to test, translation repairs
relation identification, though not uniformly across its five usable
languages; entity grounding presents no verdict-eligible gap to repair for
either system and is, descriptively, reduced by translation for one of the
two systems studied, in the evaluated setting.
(4)~The complete harness, audit protocol, prompts, and Wikidata response cache
for reproduction, released anonymously during review.

\subsection{Aim and Objectives}\label{sec:aims}
The aim of this study is to determine, at the level of individual pipeline
components rather than end-to-end task score, whether machine translation
repairs the low-resource penalty in extracting Wikidata triples from
Indic-language text. The specific objectives are:
\begin{itemize}
\item To construct a text-to-triple extraction benchmark for eight Indic
languages and an English control by deterministically re-grounding an
existing fact-to-text alignment resource in Wikidata, retaining an item only
if every one of its gold facts resolves (Algorithm~\ref{alg:regrounding}).
\item To validate that adapted benchmark as a measurement instrument through
a native-speaker audit and automated live-Wikidata cross-checking of gold
incompleteness, before trusting any score computed against it.
\item To test, under a decision table frozen before any model output was
scored, whether translating low-resource input to English repairs relation
identification, entity grounding, or both, using two open 8B-class LLMs
(Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and IndicTrans2 machine translation.
\item To decompose entity-grounding failure into retrieval misses versus
selection errors from the archived candidate sets each system was shown
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:retrieval-decomp}), rather than treating grounding as a
single undifferentiated error class.
\item To quantify uncertainty on every reported gap-recovery ratio with a
hierarchical bootstrap and to report the outcome under a
Holm-Bonferroni-corrected decision rule fixed before any data existed.
\item To examine, exploratorily and outside the pre-registered decision
table, whether the measured relation/entity dissociation holds consistently
across the study's Indo-Aryan and Dravidian language groups
(Section~\ref{sec:grouping}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Multilingual relation extraction and KG population.}
Supervised multilingual relation extraction is anchored by
RED\textsuperscript{FM}~\cite{huguetcabot2023redfm} (human-revised in 7
languages, auto-annotated SREDFM in 18) and its mREBEL extractor, whose
training coverage includes only Hindi of the eight languages here, exploited
in Section~\ref{sec:secondary} as a controlled in-coverage anchor. Earlier
monolingual end-to-end extraction~\cite{huguetcabot2021rebel} frames the
task without separating relation identification from entity grounding,
precisely the distinction under test here; LLM-era extraction reframes it as
constrained generation against a schema~\cite{josifoski2022genie} or
revisits classical RE with LLM prompting~\cite{wadhwa2023revisiting}, and
neither decomposes failure by component.

\textbf{Entity linking against Wikidata.}
Entity canonicalization here grounds mentions in
Wikidata~\cite{vrandecic2014wikidata}, an instance of multilingual entity
linking's retrieve-then-rerank design~\cite{wu2020blink}: retrieve
candidates, then disambiguate, exactly where Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-decomp}'s
decomposition shows this study's failures concentrate. Autoregressive and
cross-lingual linking systems~\cite{decao2021genre,decao2022mgenre} and
large-scale evaluations~\cite{botha2020entitylinking100} report linking
quality degrading with language coverage independent of the linking model
itself, and a Wikidata-specific survey finds most approaches lean on labels
and descriptions alone~\cite{moller2022wikidatasurvey}, precisely the
interface this paper's candidate search measures.

\textbf{Fact--text resources for Indic languages.}
XAlign~\cite{abhishek2022xalign} aligns Wikidata facts with sentences in
eight Indic languages for \emph{fact-to-text generation} and includes a
manually annotated test set. We use it in the reversed direction (sentences
as input, aligned facts as extraction gold), a construction that is ours,
not the original authors', whose validity we treat as a claim requiring
evidence (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}); its reliability for reuse as
extraction gold is itself a pre-registered secondary question.

\textbf{Translate-test versus direct multilingual inference.}
Translate-test entered cross-lingual NLP as a baseline that translating test
data outperforms other options~\cite{conneau2018xnli}, became standard
protocol across dozens of languages and tasks~\cite{hu2020xtreme,ahuja2023mega},
and persists in the LLM era: models are ``unable to leverage their full
multilingual potential when prompted in non-English
languages''~\cite{etxaniz2024think}. That literature evaluates end-to-end
scores, not \emph{which component} translation repairs, and can understate a
real risk, translation itself introduces artifacts that change what is
measured~\cite{artetxe2020translationartifacts} (Section~\ref{sec:implications}).
Our design imports the comparison into KG population and decomposes it,
using IndicTrans2~\cite{gala2023indictrans2} (all 22 scheduled Indian
languages) plus a pre-registered CometKiwi~\cite{rei2022cometkiwi}
quality-estimation check relating residual \textsc{pivot}-arm error to MT
quality, not run in this study (Section~\ref{sec:secondary}).

\textbf{Tokenization and resource proxies.}
Prior work shows the same text can cost up to fifteen times more tokens in
some languages than in English, a disparity that persists in intentionally
multilingual tokenizers~\cite{petrov2023tokenizers}. We carry tokenizer
fertility and pretraining resource share as exploratory correlates only
(Section~\ref{sec:secondary}); neither enters the primary verdict.

\subsection{Problem Statement}\label{sec:problem}
The literature above leaves a specific, testable gap open:
\begin{itemize}
\item End-to-end multilingual extractors, including
mREBEL~\cite{huguetcabot2023redfm} and its monolingual
predecessor~\cite{huguetcabot2021rebel}, score triples monolithically, so a
missed fact does not indicate whether the failure was in relation
identification or in entity grounding.
\item Translate-test evidence that English translation improves multilingual
inference~\cite{etxaniz2024think} is reported at the level of end-to-end
task scores, leaving open which sub-task of a structured extraction
pipeline actually benefits and which does not.
\item Fact-to-text alignment resources built for generation, such as
XAlign~\cite{abhishek2022xalign}, are attractive to reuse as extraction gold
because they are already human-verified, but no established reliability
estimate exists for that reuse direction.
\item Supervised multilingual extractors such as mREBEL cover only a narrow
subset of low-resource languages (Hindi alone of the eight studied here),
limiting their usefulness as baselines for broad Indic-language evaluation.
\item Tokenizer-fertility disparities across
languages~\cite{petrov2023tokenizers} are proposed as a driver of
low-resource performance gaps, but are rarely tested jointly with a
component-level decomposition of what actually fails.
\item Multilingual entity-linking evaluations typically report one aggregate
linking accuracy rather than separating what candidate retrieval fails to
surface from what disambiguation gets wrong given a correct
candidate~\cite{wu2020blink,botha2020entitylinking100}, the same
monolithic-scoring gap this paper targets specifically on the entity side.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{From Alignment Resource to Extraction Benchmark}
The manually annotated XAlign test split provides, per language, sentence-level
alignments between native-language Wikipedia text and Wikidata facts rendered
as English labels (subject, predicate, object, plus qualifiers). Two
transformations produce an extraction benchmark from it: \emph{reversal},
treating the sentence as input and its aligned facts as the triples an
extractor should recover (by construction a set of facts genuinely asserted
by the text, since the original annotation asks which facts the sentence
expresses, while inheriting XAlign's human verification), and
\emph{re-grounding}, resolving XAlign's English label strings to the
canonical Wikidata identifiers scoring requires.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:regrounding} gives the re-grounding procedure exactly as
implemented, without LLM participation and deterministic in the reproducible
sense: given the archived Wikidata response cache and the released predicate
mapping table, it yields the same benchmark exactly. Predicate labels
resolve against Wikidata's property registry by exact case-folded match,
with a 5-entry manually verified override table for the small closed set
whose labels have drifted since XAlign's snapshot (released with
per-predicate match types); a lone non-exact search candidate is accepted
but flagged for post-hoc review. Subjects resolve by retrieving name
candidates and scoring each by how many of the item's own gold facts its
live Wikidata claims corroborate, so an item's facts disambiguate its own
subject. Objects are never resolved by free search: an entity object's QID
is accepted only if it appears among the resolved subject's own claim values
for that property with a matching English label, while date and quantity
objects are normalized to a literal and accepted only if that literal
matches a claim value, then retained as a literal rather than a QID. An item
enters the benchmark only if \emph{every} fact resolves (strict retention),
so the benchmark never carries silently missing gold into recall
denominators; membership in Wikidata is thereby part of the task definition,
and NIL detection (entities Wikidata lacks) is out of scope by construction.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Deterministic gold re-grounding: XAlign fact labels to Wikidata IDs}
\label{alg:regrounding}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\footnotesize
\Require XAlign item: sentence, fact list $F=\{(s,p,o)\}$ of English label strings
\Ensure gold triple set $G$, or \textsc{drop}(reason)
\State $G \gets \emptyset$
\ForAll{distinct predicate label $p$ in $F$}
  \State $P_p \gets$ exact case-folded match against the Wikidata property registry
  \If{$P_p=\bot$ \textbf{and} $p$ is a known drifted label (5-entry table)}
    \State $P_p \gets$ verified override
  \ElsIf{$P_p=\bot$ \textbf{and} exactly one non-exact search candidate exists}
    \State $P_p \gets$ that candidate \Comment{flagged for post-hoc review}
  \EndIf
  \If{$P_p=\bot$} \State \Return \textsc{drop}(\textsc{predicate-unresolved}) \EndIf
\EndFor
\ForAll{distinct subject label $s$ in $F$}
  \State $C \gets$ top-8 name-search candidates for $s$
  \State $Q_s \gets \arg\max_{q\in C}$ corroboration$(q, s\text{'s facts})$ \Comment{claims of $q$ that support a fact}
  \If{$\max$ corroboration $=0$} \State \Return \textsc{drop}(\textsc{subject-unresolved}) \EndIf
\EndFor
\ForAll{fact $(s,p,o)\in F$ with resolved $Q_s,P_p$}
  \If{$o$ normalizes to a date/quantity literal $o'$}
    \If{$o'$ matches no claim of $Q_s$ on $P_p$} \State \Return \textsc{drop}(\textsc{literal-not-in-claims}) \EndIf
  \Else
    \State $o'\gets$ QID in $Q_s$'s $P_p$-claims whose English label matches $o$ (else $\bot$)
    \If{$o'=\bot$} \State \Return \textsc{drop}(\textsc{object-unresolved}) \EndIf
  \EndIf
  \State $G \gets G \cup \{(Q_s,P_p,o')\}$
\EndFor
\State \Return $G$ \Comment{the item enters the benchmark only here, with every fact resolved}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Component Metrics and Three-Way Prediction Classification}
Every run is scored with relation-F1 (property identifiers only), entity-F1
(subject and object QIDs only), and full-triple F1, computed in the strict
(Wikidata-ID) form throughout. A relaxed, surface-form variant of the same
three metrics was pre-registered but not computed for this draft, the same
treatment the paper gives other un-run pre-registered items
(Section~\ref{sec:secondary}); nothing below depends on it. Separating the
relation and entity components is what allows the hypothesis to be tested at
all: a triple wrong only in its object QID counts against entity grounding,
not relation understanding.

Because gold reuse can understate model precision (a model may extract a
true fact the aligned gold simply does not contain), every predicted triple
is classified three ways: in gold (exact tuple match); absent from gold but
verifiable as a live Wikidata statement (gold incompleteness, credited in
adjusted precision), checked against the same cached Wikidata access layer
as re-grounding under literal-matching rules frozen with the prompts before
any scoring; or absent from Wikidata entirely (a hallucination candidate,
analyzed taxonomically in Section~\ref{sec:secondary}). The exact procedure
is released with the harness (\texttt{wikidata\_check.py}). Predictions
lacking a resolvable subject QID, property PID, or object are set aside
before this split rather than forced into one of the three classes (pooled
across all 34 cells, 19.4\% of predictions); already scored as errors under
the primary metrics, their exclusion affects only this diagnostic's
denominator, not precision.

\subsection{Instrument Validation}
Two independent checks estimate how far the adapted gold can be trusted. A
native-speaker audit protocol, released with the benchmark, covers one full
language subset (Tamil; 656 source items) and records two judgments per item
against written guidelines: whether each gold fact is genuinely asserted by
the sentence (gold validity), and which sentence-asserted facts are absent
from the gold (gold incompleteness, the miss rate claim C2 tests), each
cross-checked against Wikidata; the auditor is treated as a measurement
device, with the miss-rate threshold (5\%) fixed in the pre-registration.
The automated class-(ii) rate separately provides a scalable per-language
estimate of gold incompleteness for all eight languages. The pre-registration
commits to publishing whichever way these measurements fall: a miss rate at
or above threshold, together with a positive automated class-(ii) rate in at
least seven of eight languages, supports the claim that generation-oriented
gold understates extraction precision; a miss rate below threshold supports
the pre-registered inverse claim that such reuse is reliable.

The automated estimate came back decisively below threshold: across all 34
(system, arm, language) cells, the share of not-in-gold predictions
verifiable as live Wikidata statements never reaches 5\% (maximum 4.8\%, one
cell; median 1.3\%; pooled 389 of 30{,}171, 1.3\%). The frozen rule keys the
reliability determination itself to the native-speaker audit's miss rate
against the same threshold; below it, strict re-grounding yields a benchmark
whose precision estimates are reliable, and what the models predict beyond
gold is overwhelmingly not knowledge the gold failed to record. A
drop-classification audit of the retention step points the same way: in a
pre-registered 50-item sample of dropped source items, all 50 are consistent
with knowledge-base drift, and exactly one item in the full 660-drop pool
exhibits the procedural signature the classification was designed to catch
(an unparsed unit quantity). The native-speaker audit itself is scheduled
prior to submission; its miss rate, against the same 5\% threshold, is
reported here alongside the automated bound once complete.

\subsection{Pre-registration}
All decision thresholds, statistical procedures, exclusion rules, and the
outcome-to-claim mapping used in Section~\ref{sec:test} were frozen in a
version-controlled pre-registration~\cite{vanmiltenburg2021preregistering}
committed before any model output was scored; the scoring harness refuses to
run unless it can verify the frozen commit in its own history. The anonymized repository (including the frozen
pre-registration file, prompts, audit protocol, the archived Wikidata

\subsection{Arms and Evaluation Pipeline}\label{sec:arms}
Each system runs \textsc{direct} (original language in,
extract-then-canonicalize) and \textsc{pivot} (translation to English via
IndicTrans2's distilled \texttt{indic-en-dist-200M} checkpoint, then the
identical English pipeline) over the same low-resource items, item-paired by
construction, plus the \textsc{english} control slice, which by construction
contains different sentences (pairing note, Section~\ref{sec:grr}). Each
item passes through an arm-specific input transform, open-vocabulary
extraction with the response archived before any parsing, and per-triple
canonicalization against retrieved candidates (the full per-item procedure
is released as \texttt{run\_matrix.py} with the harness). Using the smallest
released IndicTrans2 checkpoint rather than its 1B flagship means the
relation-repair result below is a \emph{lower bound} on what a stronger
pivot engine would deliver, and correspondingly that the entity-side damage
cannot be claimed independent of MT quality (Section~\ref{sec:implications}).

A mechanical parsing artifact surfaced at first scoring, Llama-3.1 echoes
the canonicalization template's header line before answering, handled with
a dated pre-reparse amendment (both pre- and post-amendment scores are
released; Section~\ref{sec:implications} states the implication for
cross-model comparison). Relation canonicalization is closed-set: the
canonicalization prompt carries the inventory of properties attested in the
adapted gold, so systems \emph{select} rather than generate property
identifiers, and absolute relation-F1 is therefore not open-inventory
performance (Section~\ref{sec:implications}); extraction itself remains
open-vocabulary.

\subsection{Entity Retrieval versus Selection}\label{sec:retrieval-decomp}
Entity canonicalization is retrieval-mediated: a mention is grounded by
choosing among at most eight candidates returned by Wikidata's search API
(queried in the source language with an English top-up in \textsc{direct},
and in English in \textsc{pivot} and \textsc{english}), with the instruction
prompts themselves held constant in English across all arms and languages;
only the passage varies. Retrieval is thus a component of measured entity
grounding, not a controlled-away nuisance: a native-script mention can fail
at retrieval before the model ever chooses. This asymmetry was presumed
conservative for the hypothesis at design time; Algorithm~\ref{alg:retrieval-decomp}
decomposes entity errors into retrieval misses versus selection errors from
the archived candidate sets, and the decomposition below voids that
presumption for one of the two systems.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Entity retrieval-versus-selection decomposition (per system, arm, language)}
\label{alg:retrieval-decomp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\footnotesize
\Require scored items with archived subject-candidate lists ($\le 8$ each, from the harness's stage-2 retrieval step) and gold subject QID
\Ensure subject recall@8, selection-given-retrieved
\State $retrieved \gets 0$, $selected \gets 0$, $n \gets 0$
\ForAll{scored item}
  \State $n \gets n+1$
  \If{gold subject QID $\in$ archived candidate list}
    \State $retrieved \gets retrieved+1$
    \If{model's chosen subject QID $=$ gold subject QID} \State $selected \gets selected+1$ \EndIf
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $retrieved/n$ \Comment{subject recall@8}, $\;selected/retrieved$ \Comment{selection given retrieved}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The decomposition inverts the presumed direction of the retrieval asymmetry
for Qwen3-8B, the system driving the paper's usable relation-repair
contrasts. Measured subject-candidate recall@8 is \emph{higher} in
\textsc{direct} than in \textsc{pivot} for seven of eight languages (e.g.,
Hindi $.612$ vs.\ $.518$; Kannada is the sole exception, at parity, $.215$
vs.\ $.217$; full values in Figure~\ref{fig:retrieval}): the native-language
query with English top-up retrieves gold entities that English-side search
on translated mentions loses. Llama-3.1-8B does not replicate this
direction, its \textsc{pivot} recall@8 exceeds \textsc{direct} in seven of
eight languages (Marathi the sole exception), so the mechanism is evidenced
for one system and does not generalize across both by construction.
Selection, by contrast, is high wherever retrieval succeeds in both systems,
though not uniformly near-ceiling: given the gold entity among the
candidates, Qwen3-8B selects it in 92.9--99.5\% of calls per cell, and
Llama-3.1-8B in 76.2--94.7\%. Entity grounding here is therefore
retrieval-bound rather than selection-bound for the system whose behavior
licenses the paper's mechanistic claim, and the \textsc{english} control is
itself retrieval-limited (subject recall@8 $=.549$, Qwen3-8B).

\subsection{Gap Recovery Ratio}\label{sec:grr}
For metric $m \in \{\text{relation-F1}, \text{entity-F1}\}$ and each
(language, system) cell,
\[
\mathrm{GRR}_m \;=\; \frac{m_{\textsc{pivot}} - m_{\textsc{direct}}}
                          {m_{\textsc{english}} - m_{\textsc{direct}}},
\]
reported unclipped (a clipped-to-$[0,1]$ variant was pre-registered but is
not in the released score report; nothing below depends on it). Cells whose
baseline gap $(m_{\textsc{english}} - m_{\textsc{direct}})$ is under 8 F1
points are excluded from verdicts as underpowered (logged and reported); if
a metric retains fewer than five usable languages, its verdict is declared
underpowered and only CIs are reported. Algorithm~\ref{alg:grr} gives the
exclusion rule and per-cell verdict test exactly as implemented; uncertainty
comes from a hierarchical bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples, 95\% CIs, released
as \texttt{bootstrap.py}): item-paired for \textsc{pivot}-vs-\textsc{direct},
independent resampling for the unpaired \textsc{english} term. Because the
\textsc{english} ceiling uses XAlign's English slice (same domain and
register, different sentences), the design additionally pre-registers a
robustness probe that machine-translates a sample of English gold items
\emph{into} each study language and scores the \textsc{direct} arm on this
synthetic-parallel text against the same gold, giving a fully paired
denominator check (with the translationese caveat stated); prepared but not
run for this draft (Section~\ref{sec:implications}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Gap Recovery Ratio, exclusion rule, and per-cell verdict}
\label{alg:grr}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\footnotesize
\Require corpus F1 scores $m_{\textsc{direct}}, m_{\textsc{pivot}}, m_{\textsc{english}}$ for one (language, system, metric) cell
\Ensure $\mathrm{GRR}$ or \textsc{excluded}; verdict $\in\{\textsc{repaired},\textsc{partial},\textsc{not-repaired}\}$
\State $gap \gets m_{\textsc{english}} - m_{\textsc{direct}}$
\If{$gap \times 100 < 8$} \State \Return \textsc{excluded} \Comment{underpowered cell, logged not scored} \EndIf
\State $\mathrm{GRR} \gets (m_{\textsc{pivot}} - m_{\textsc{direct}}) / gap$
\State $\mathrm{CI} \gets$ 95\% hierarchical bootstrap on $\mathrm{GRR}$ ($B=10{,}000$; item-paired direct/pivot, independent english)
\If{$\mathrm{GRR}\geq 0.75$ \textbf{and} $0\in \mathrm{CI}(m_{\textsc{english}}-m_{\textsc{pivot}})$}
  \State verdict $\gets \textsc{repaired}$
\ElsIf{$0.35\leq \mathrm{GRR}<0.75$ \textbf{and} $0\notin \mathrm{CI}(m_{\textsc{pivot}}-m_{\textsc{direct}})$}
  \State verdict $\gets \textsc{partial}$
\Else
  \State verdict $\gets \textsc{not-repaired}$
\EndIf
\State \Return $\mathrm{GRR}$, $\mathrm{CI}$, verdict
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Decision Table and Verdict}\label{sec:decision}
The pre-registered table maps repair patterns to claims. In brief: relation
repair requires $\mathrm{GRR} \geq 0.75$ with the residual
(\textsc{english}$-$\textsc{pivot}) CI including 0; non-repair is
$\mathrm{GRR} < 0.35$ or an improvement CI including 0; the intermediate band
($0.35 \leq \mathrm{GRR} < 0.75$ with the improvement CI excluding 0) is
scored as partial repair. The hypothesis is confirmed only when relation-F1
repairs while entity-F1 either does not repair or repairs only partially, and
the asymmetry $\mathrm{GRR}_{\text{rel}} - \mathrm{GRR}_{\text{ent}} \geq 0.30$
with a CI excluding 0. Cross-language consistency uses an exact one-sided
sign test in the hypothesis' pre-specified direction (all-but-at-most-one of
the usable languages, exact binomial $p<0.05$; sidedness fixed by a dated,
pre-scoring amendment recorded in the frozen file's log). The primary family
(2 metrics $\times$ 2 systems = 4 tests) is Holm-Bonferroni corrected; system
disagreement on a verdict row resolves to the weaker outcome. Both
falsification branches carry their own pre-registered claims: repair of
\emph{both} components would evidence an English-bound knowledge interface
in current open LLMs; relation-F1 itself failing to repair redirects the
explanation toward entity-coverage structure, tested by popularity-quartile
stratification over the retained items' popularity distribution (dropped
items are unscorable, so the quartile claim is scoped to what the benchmark
retains). Any other outcome is reported as mixed: the continuous GRR profile
is published without forcing a headline verdict, led instead by the
gold-reliability finding (Section~\ref{sec:instrument}).

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Usable cells.} The 8-point gap rule excludes more of the grid than
anticipated, for two informative reasons. On entity-F1, the \textsc{english}

grounding is retrieval-bound even in English, so no language shows a
measurable entity gap for Qwen3 (0 usable; Llama-3.1: 4, below the 5-language

\section{Secondary Analyses}\label{sec:secondary}

This section reports the remaining pre-registered secondary and exploratory
analyses, several of which resolve to a confirmed drop or omission rather
than a positive result, plus one post-hoc exploratory grouping raised during
review.

\paragraph{Popularity stratification (pre-registered for the B3 branch;
reported descriptively).} Entity-occurrence error rates in the \textsc{pivot}
arm are flat across sitelink-popularity (the count of Wikimedia sitelinks
the entity has, a notability proxy) quartiles of the retained inventory:
bottom-vs-top ratio $1.03$ (Qwen3; $.706$ vs.\ $.688$) and $0.89$
(Llama-3.1), far from the $1.5$ criterion. Failures do not concentrate on
obscure entities (famous ones fail almost as often), consistent with the
retrieval-bound account and not with an entity-coverage account.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and composition checks (pre-registered).} Excluding
every item touching the two audit-flagged predicate mappings
(P112, P118) shifts no usable GRR by more than $0.05$ (largest shift:
Marathi, $1.334 \to 1.293$). The retention-vs-GRR association across the
five usable cells is $\rho = 0.60$ (n$=5$, descriptive only, stated with the
retention-differential caveat). GRR CI half-widths run $0.12$--$0.16$
(Llama-3.1 cells) and $0.22$--$0.54$ (Qwen3 relation cells, wide where GRR is
large); per-cell resample counts after the excluded-draw rule are released
with the report.

\paragraph{Supervised-baseline inversion test (C3).} The pre-registered scope
gate recorded a drop: mREBEL, the supervised baseline, has training coverage
that includes only Hindi of the eight study languages
(Section~\ref{sec:related}), so an inversion test against it would be
uninformative for seven of the eight comparisons, a decision recorded at
the gate, before any such run.

\paragraph{Exploratory items not executed.} Three further pre-registered
exploratory items were not run in this study and are omitted rather than
partially reported: the C4 tokenizer-fertility-versus-resource-proxy
correlate, the C5 fragment on repair-pattern stability across model scale,
and the QE-correlation sharpening check relating CometKiwi estimates to
residual \textsc{pivot}-arm error.

\paragraph{Hallucination-candidate structure (C6).} Pooled across all 34
(system, arm, language) cells, 30{,}171 predicted triples were absent from
gold; of these, 389 (1.3\%) were verified as true live-Wikidata statements
(Section~\ref{sec:instrument}), and the remainder are hallucination
candidates. Their taxonomic structure is released with the error tables; this
is a descriptive dimension of the error analysis, not a claim.

\subsection{Language-Family Grouping (Exploratory)}\label{sec:grouping}
This grouping analysis is exploratory and post-hoc. Language family, script,
and resource tier are not variables in the frozen pre-registration
(Section~\ref{sec:decision}); no grouping hypothesis, threshold, or test was
specified before the data were seen, no amendment governs it, and it
contributes to no verdict. It is reported because a null result is
informative: whether the pre-registered dissociation is an artifact of one
language family.

The eight study languages split by genealogical family into Indo-Aryan
(Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati) and Dravidian (Tamil, Telugu, Kannada,
Malayalam). An exact permutation test over all 70 possible 4-versus-4
relabelings, the exact null distribution at this $n$, finds nothing: across
20 such tests (both systems, both arms, the relation and entity deltas, and
recall@8), the smallest $p$ is $.1143$, against a minimum attainable
two-sided $p$ of $.0286$ at this sample size; the dissociation itself is far
from separating by family ($p=.771$ Qwen3, $p=.543$ Llama-3.1). Direction is
identical in both families (Qwen3 relation-F1 gain $+.127$ Indo-Aryan versus
$+.113$ Dravidian; entity-F1 loss $-.062$ versus $-.041$), and Dravidian
alone contains both the lowest usable GRR in the study (Kannada, Table~\ref{tab:grr})
and the highest (Malayalam): within-family spread swamps the
between-family difference.

What does correlate with entity grounding is retrieval coverage, not
genealogy: subject recall@8 (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-decomp}) correlates
with entity-F1 at Spearman $\rho=+.881$ (Qwen3 \textsc{direct}), $+.738$
(Qwen3 \textsc{pivot}), $+.738$ (Llama-3.1 \textsc{direct}), and $+.857$
(Llama-3.1 \textsc{pivot}), stratifying the eight languages into a
high-retrieval group (Hindi, Marathi, Malayalam) and a low-retrieval group
(Kannada, Tamil, Gujarati) that cuts across both families, independently
corroborating the paper's retrieval-bound mechanism on a dimension the
decision table never touched. (Devanagari Hindi and Marathi do show higher
Qwen3 recall@8 than the other six scripts, but are also the two
highest-Wikidata-coverage languages studied; script and resource level are
confounded at $n=2$, so this is not reported as a script effect.)

This mechanism is plausibly a property of how well a language's entity
names are represented in the knowledge base's search index, not of its
genealogy, so it is expected wherever that representation is weak. The
closest verified evidence comes from multilingual entity linking at scale,
which degrades with language coverage independent of the linking model
itself~\cite{botha2020entitylinking100}; from named-entity work under
comparable scarcity in Sub-Saharan African
languages~\cite{adelani2021masakhaner}; and from the low-resource
machine-translation landscape the pivot arm depends
on~\cite{nllbteam2024scaling}. The cheapest extension within the Indic
family is the two families this study does not cover, since a 24-language,
four-family Indic resource already exists~\cite{doddapaneni2023indicxtreme};
we did not add a further experimental language because XAlign, the source of
this study's gold, has no annotated split outside these eight languages and
English, so any addition would use a different annotation scheme and
property inventory, making its numbers incomparable rather than a genuine
extension. Whether the mechanism replicates elsewhere is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\section{Implications and Limitations}\label{sec:implications}
% For practitioners (translate first?) and benchmark builders (gold-reuse reliability).
% Limitations: single-family breadth, gold reuse caveats, audit on one language.

The measured profile yields a component-level rule for practitioners rather
than a blanket one. Where \emph{which relations} a low-resource text asserts
is the quantity of interest, translating first is worth it: repair is
directionally uniform across usable languages, exceeding the English
ceiling in one. Where \emph{which entities} are involved is the quantity of
interest, translation is the wrong lever for the system whose retrieval
pattern we measured, since it damages the retrieval path grounding
bottlenecks on; effort is better spent on the retrieval interface itself
(native-label indexing, cross-lingual entity search), as selection is high
wherever the right candidate is retrieved (92.9--99.5\% for that system,
76.2--94.7\% for the other). For benchmark builders: generation-oriented
alignment gold can plausibly be reused for extraction evaluation if it is
strictly re-grounded against the live KB first, at the measured cost of a
$\sim$11\% drop rate that is overwhelmingly KB drift, with precision
estimates our automated incompleteness bound suggests are not materially
understated, pending audit confirmation.

\emph{Limitations.} The study covers open LLMs of a single size class under
4-bit quantization; conclusions about scaling rest only on the labeled
exploratory fragment. Both models were served at a 4096-token context, well
under Qwen3-8B's native window and Llama-3.1's (Table~\ref{tab:params}); the
limit was binding on at least one item, forcing the compact candidate
rendering used uniformly across arms, and Qwen3's thinking mode was disabled
for latency parity with Llama-3.1, so its scores here are not comparable to
published thinking-mode Qwen3 benchmarks. The gold inherits XAlign's
person-biography domain and its 2020--2021 snapshot, mitigated but not
eliminated by strict retention and live-Wikidata cross-checking. The
native-speaker audit covers one of the eight languages; the other seven rely
on the automated incompleteness estimate. The \textsc{pivot} arm uses one MT
engine, IndicTrans2's distilled 200M checkpoint rather than its 1B flagship
(Section~\ref{sec:arms}); the QE correlation would bound, but was not run to
remove, MT-engine and MT-quality specificity. Relation canonicalization is
scored as selection over the gold's closed property inventory, identical
across arms and languages; open-inventory canonicalization is a harder task,
so absolute relation-F1 does not transfer to open-world settings, though the
closed set leaves the arm and language \emph{contrasts} untouched. The
Llama-3.1 template-echo artifact (Section~\ref{sec:arms}) shows
format-compliance parsing is itself an instrument risk for cross-model
comparison. The synthetic-parallel denominator probe (Section~\ref{sec:grr})
was not run in time for this draft; until it runs, the
\textsc{english}-ceiling pairing caveat rests on the within-language pairing
argument alone. The language-family grouping (Section~\ref{sec:grouping}) is
exploratory and severely underpowered; it should not be read as a test of
any family-level hypothesis. Finally, each cell reflects a single seeded
decoding run rather than repeated sampling, so the results carry no estimate
of decoding variance.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This study asked whether the low-resource penalty in extracting Wikidata
triples from Indic-language text is a failure of relation understanding or
of entity canonicalization, and answered it with a pre-registered,
component-separated measurement rather than an end-to-end score. The
evidence dissociates the two: translation repairs relation identification
(Qwen3-8B, five usable languages, $p=.031$ uncorrected; descriptively 7/8 for
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) but presents no verdict-eligible entity-grounding gap
under the frozen rule, since the English ceiling is itself near-floor for
both systems, and descriptively reduces Qwen3-8B's entity-F1 in 7/8
languages. The retrieval-versus-selection decomposition traces this to
weaker retrieval on the translated query, not to worse selection, a
mechanism specific to Qwen3-8B that does not replicate on Llama-3.1-8B. The
benchmark this measurement relies on is a reuse of generation-oriented
alignment gold, whose reliability we treated as a claim requiring evidence
rather than an assumption; the automated cross-check keeps the
not-in-gold-but-true rate below the pre-registered 5\% threshold in every
cell, pending native-speaker confirmation.

The practical implication is component-specific: systems that need relation
identification from low-resource text benefit from translating first;
systems that need entities grounded should instead invest in the retrieval
interface itself (native-label indexing, cross-lingual entity search), since
the bottleneck measured here sits there, not in the model's ability to
choose correctly once the right candidate is in view.

\emph{Future work.} The synthetic-parallel denominator probe and the
CometKiwi quality-estimation correlation are prepared but not run; a
model-size sweep would show whether the Qwen-specific retrieval mechanism is
a property of this model family or of scale, and repeated sampling per cell
would let decoding variance enter the uncertainty budget. Beyond this
study's two systems, the retrieval-bound account of entity grounding
suggests a concrete engineering target: a native-script-aware entity index
built for cross-lingual search may repair grounding in a way translation
itself cannot.
